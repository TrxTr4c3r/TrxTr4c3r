A curated, field-tested directory of open-source intelligence tools for crypto forensics, digital investigations, and threat hunting. Catalogue version v2026.05; 170 tools across 23 categories (all entries verified 2026-05 unless a note says otherwise).

OPSEC legend: **Safe** = low exposure; **Caution** = the tool transmits/logs your query to a third party (source tag "OPSEC"); **High** = strong exposure (hostile-jurisdiction hosting, contact upload, face search, etc.). Tool inclusion is not endorsement; OPSEC tags are advisory, not guarantees; verify findings independently.

## The three-piece investigator workflow (framing)

Every investigation breaks into three pieces (per the stack article):

1. **Investigation & Tracing**, heavyweights (TRM, Chainalysis, Elliptic) + next-tier tracers (Global Ledger, CipherOwl, MistTrack) + freemium graphs (Breadcrumbs, MetaSleuth) + free attribution/clustering (Arkham, Bubblemaps) + base-layer explorers (Etherscan, OKLink, mempool.space). Bridges/swaps via dedicated + aggregate explorers; instant-swaps often need temporal+amount matching or direct outreach.
2. **Identification & OSINT**, the hardest half: link on-chain address -> entity -> real person (Web3 -> Web2). Exchange KYC is the gold standard but LE-gated. When it is not available, pivot via domain/infra intel (Validin, Silent Push, Shodan/Censys, crt.sh), tech-stack IDs (BuiltWith/Wappalyzer), person pivots (Epieos, OSINT Industries, Holehe), and scam DBs; preserve with Wayback/archive.today. Document everything (Obsidian/Notion/Hunchly/Maltego).
3. **Freezing, Blacklisting & Recovery**, report/blacklist/flag wallets so institutions can freeze; issuer freezes (Tether/Circle), T3 FCU, TRM Beacon; LE-only coordination via Deconflict/Kodex; recovery via Asset Reality/DigitalMint.

# Court Records & Litigation by Country, added 2026-08-05

*Why court records belong in a crypto-forensics catalogue: filings are the cheapest bridge from a pseudonym to a person. Civil complaints, indictments, and above all US civil-forfeiture filings routinely name subject wallet addresses in full, sitting alongside the parties' legal names, street addresses, employers, aliases, co-defendants, and counsel, and they are public the moment they are docketed. That makes a docket search both an identity source and, in the US, a direct on-chain attribution source. Bankruptcy and receivership dockets are separately where creditor lists, asset schedules, and exchange subpoenas surface.*

*Three caveats before you lean on any of this. (1) An index is not the filing: most systems return metadata and you still pay for or formally request the document. (2) Coverage skews hard toward appellate and superior courts in most countries; trial-level records are often offline, in-person only, or sealed. (3) Absence of a record is not evidence of absence, and that is sharpest with RECAP, which holds only what somebody already bought. Link-checked 2026-08-05 unless a row says otherwise; confirm the current URL before relying on any entry.*

## Working a docket in a crypto case

1. **Pick the jurisdiction from the fiat off-ramp or the corporate entity, not from the wallet.** Chain has no venue. The exchange, the shell company, or the victim does.
2. **Free index first, free archive second, paid pull last.** PCL or WorldLII or e-Justice to locate; CourtListener, judyrecords, BAILII, CanLII to read; PACER or a court fee only when the document is not already free somewhere.
3. **Pull the complaint, indictment, or asset schedule, not the docket summary.** Wallet addresses, exchange account numbers, and street addresses live in the exhibits. The case caption gives you almost nothing.
4. **Copy any address out of a filing in full, then verify it on an explorer before it enters a case file.** A court document is a third-party assertion, so tag it `#unverified` until you have the transaction, block, and exact value from an explorer (Gate 0).
5. **Cite case number, court, filing date, and document number.** A screenshot of a docket page with no case number is not an exhibit.
6. **Remember PACER searches are attributable.** You register under an identity and every query is billed to it. If the subject is a party who monitors their own docket, treat access as a disclosed action and plan accordingly.

Every other platform named in that source (Chainalysis Reactor, Elliptic Investigator, TRM Labs, Breadcrumbs, Arkham, GraphSense, Blockchair, Etherscan) is already catalogued above. Read alongside Three Functions of Forensic Tooling: in litigation the tool has to be chosen for the task and the admissibility bar, not for what you already have a licence to.*

| Tool | What it is | Access | Website | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CipherTrace | Blockchain analytics and tracing platform, now part of Mastercard | enterprise | ciphertrace.com | Named alongside Chainalysis and Elliptic as one of the platforms most likely to appear in litigation. Same black box caveat: the attribution is proprietary, so a label needs independent corroboration before it becomes a finding |
| Learn Me A Bitcoin | Reference site explaining Bitcoin internals (transaction structure, UTXOs, keys, script types, Taproot) with worked byte-level examples | free | learnmeabitcoin.com | Not an investigation tool, a translation tool. The place to go when a report has to explain what a UTXO or a script type actually is to a non-technical reader, and the fastest route to understanding a transaction you are reading raw |

# Additions from international-guidance sources, ingested 2026-09-14

*From three ingested sources: Alexander Browder, "Confronting the Illicit-Finance Hydra in Crypto Markets" (Henry Jackson Society / Centre for Resilient Society, March 2026); GAFILAT, "Guide on Relevant Aspects and Appropriate Steps for the Investigation, Identification, Seizure, and Confiscation of Virtual Assets" (December 2021); OSCE Mission to Skopje / Academy for Judges and Public Prosecutors, "Handbook for Dealing with Virtual Currencies in Criminal Proceedings" (Ristoska & Veternik, undated, cites legislation to late 2021). Source PDFs in knowledge-base/_sources/. Every other platform named across the three (Chainalysis, Elliptic, TRM Labs, Maltego, Shodan, Namechk, TinEye, and the T3 Financial Crime Unit) is already catalogued above.*

| Tool | What it is | Access | Website | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Coin ATM Radar | Global map and directory of crypto ATMs and kiosks, searchable by area and by operator, with fees and supported assets | free | coinatmradar.com | The enumeration step in Working a Crypto ATM as a Physical Pivot. Lets you list the candidate machines in an area before you have a single transaction, which is what makes a records request to the operator scopeable. Listings are operator-supplied and go stale, so confirm the machine exists before building a surveillance plan around it |
| WalletExplorer | Bitcoin address clustering service that groups addresses into named wallets and labels many exchanges and large services | free | walletexplorer.com | Useful free cross-check on a commercial platform's clustering for Bitcoin. Its labels and clusters are its own and it has not been meaningfully maintained for years, so treat it as a lead generator rather than a source. Never carry a WalletExplorer label into a finding without independent corroboration |
| Basel Open Intelligence | Open platform from the Basel Institute on Governance covering money laundering and terrorist financing risk by country, with the Basel AML Index | free, registration | baselgovernance.org | Jurisdiction risk context for scoping and for the risk paragraph of a report. Country-level risk, not entity-level: it tells you what kind of counterparty environment you are in, and nothing about the specific service in front of you |
| Dark.fail / TNO Dark Web Monitor | Dark.fail is a verified-mirror index of onion services; the TNO Dark Web Monitor is a research-grade darknet monitoring platform used by law enforcement | dark.fail free, TNO restricted | dark.fail | Named in the GAFILAT guide as routes into darknet market infrastructure. **Do not open either from this workspace.** Onion indexes sit squarely inside the never-open-an-investigation-URL rule, and dark.fail's whole function is resolving addresses for services you should only reach from a designated isolated environment. Use for naming a market, not for reaching one |
| EJCN Virtual Currencies Guide / Europol VASP contact list | Guidance from the European Judicial Cybercrime Network plus a Europol-maintained contact list of exchanges and compliant entities for law enforcement and judicial authorities | restricted, law enforcement and judiciary only | europol.europa.eu | The practical answer to "who do I actually write to, and is this service real". The contact list is the counter-measure to Approaching a VASP Can Tip Off the Target: it tells you whether the service has a genuine compliance function before you send anything. Access runs through a national single point of contact, not through the public site |
