# Recovery Jurisdictions and Crypto Case Law

<span style={{ fontFamily: 'var(--font-space-mono), monospace', fontSize: 13, opacity: 0.7 }}>`reference`</span>

## Core idea
Jurisdiction selection decides whether an order is worth having. Two things drive the choice: whether the courts there already have crypto jurisprudence to build on, and whether an order made there can actually be enforced where the assets sit. Elegant law in the wrong forum recovers nothing.

**Citations below are as given by the source and have not been independently verified here. Check every one before it goes near a filing.**

## Components
**United States.** Venues: SDNY for exchange matters, N.D. Cal. for tech familiarity, E.D. Va. for speed on emergency relief. Advantages: ex parte asset freeze via TRO, nationwide effect of federal orders, broad third-party discovery under Rule 45, strong contempt powers. Cited jurisprudence: *SEC v. Telegram Group* (S.D.N.Y. 2020) on jurisdiction over token offerings; *United States v. Gratkowski* (5th Cir. 2020) on limited privacy expectation in blockchain records; *Kleiman v. Wright* (S.D. Fla. 2021) on bitcoin as property. Strategy: lean on securities aspects to draw SEC involvement, use wire fraud for expansive jurisdiction, consider parallel civil forfeiture, and route international evidence through MLAT.

**United Kingdom.** Venues: Business and Property Courts, Commercial Court, Chancery Division for proprietary claims and tracing. Advantages: worldwide freezing orders with extraterritorial effect, Bankers Trust disclosure, Norwich Pharmacal third-party disclosure. Cited jurisprudence: *AA v Persons Unknown* [2019] EWHC 3556 (Comm) recognising bitcoin as property; *Ion Science Ltd v Persons Unknown* (2020) on jurisdiction over crypto fraud; *Fetch.ai Ltd v Persons Unknown* [2021] EWHC 2254 (Comm) on exchange-based recovery.

**Singapore.** Venues: Singapore International Commercial Court, High Court Commercial List. Advantages: established proprietary injunctions over cryptocurrency, expedited disclosure, strategic position for Asian exchange enforcement. Cited jurisprudence: *B2C2 Ltd v Quoine Pte Ltd* [2019] SGHC(I) 03 on smart contract enforcement; *CLM v CLN* [2022] SGHC 46 on proprietary injunctions for cryptocurrency; *Bybit Fintech Limited* [2023] on regulatory framework application.

**European Union.** Jurisprudence varies by member state, with the CJEU *Hedqvist* case on VAT treatment as the main pan-EU reference. Watch MiCA implementation timing, jurisdiction-specific freezing mechanisms, and the data protection implications of blockchain tracing. Civil law systems approach proprietary remedies differently from common law, which changes what tracing arguments are available.

**UAE (DIFC and ADGM).** DIFC and ADGM courts are common law systems with strong freezing remedies; onshore UAE federal courts offer expedited financial crime processes. Enforcement efficiency is relationship-dependent and technical sophistication varies by forum. Increasingly important for regional exchange and OTC coverage.

**Hong Kong.** High Court Commercial List. Strong common law freezing and disclosure remedies, useful as a gateway for Chinese exchange pressure. Cited jurisprudence: *Yan Yu Ying v Leung Wing Hei* [2022] HKCFI 1660 on account of profits; *Nico Constantijn Antonius Samara v Stive Jean Paul Dan* [2022] HKCFI 1254 on freezing injunctions.

**The common law advantage.** Proprietary tracing, constructive trusts over commingled funds, unjust enrichment as a fallback, and account of profits are available in common law systems and largely are not in civil law ones. Where funds are commingled, that difference often decides the forum.

## When to use
When advising on where a claim should be brought, or when scoping which jurisdictions a trace needs to cover to keep the legal options open.

## Avoid when
Do not select a forum for the quality of its case law alone. Pick for enforcement effectiveness first, which usually means following the exchanges. Note also that this snapshot dates from mid-2025 and crypto jurisprudence moves quickly.
