# Calibrate Your Testimony Language

<span style={{ fontFamily: 'var(--font-space-mono), monospace', fontSize: 13, opacity: 0.7 }}>`tactic`</span>

## Core idea
Every sentence in a report or on the stand should describe either an action you took or an observation you made, never what something means. Meaning is opinion, and opinion belongs only to a designated expert. Anchor your language in plain action: what you did, what you observed, and where the information came from.

## Components
Phrasings to avoid as a fact witness:
- **Forensic terms of art:** "consistent with," "indicative of," "characteristic of."
- **Probabilistic language without disclosed methodology:** "very likely," "almost certainly."
- **Conclusory shortcuts:** "this is a mixer," "these addresses belong to the same person."

Corroboration phrasing for Layer 2 findings: say "I built a spreadsheet of 47 co-spending transactions linking the addresses; the tool's cluster output corroborates this finding" (your evidence, the tool corroborates), not "the tool clusters these addresses" (an uncorroborated tool conclusion).

## When to use
Drafting any report headed for court, and preparing for the stand.

## Example
Replace the present-tense, unqualified "the address holds 12 BTC" with the temporally anchored "the address held 12 BTC as of block 812,453, recorded at 14:32 UTC on 24 May 2026." The qualifier survives the defence question "does it still hold that today?"
