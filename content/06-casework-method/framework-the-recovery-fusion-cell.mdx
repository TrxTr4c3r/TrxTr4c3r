# The Recovery Fusion Cell

<span style={{ fontFamily: 'var(--font-space-mono), monospace', fontSize: 13, opacity: 0.7 }}>`framework`</span>

## Core idea
A recovery needs lawyers, analysts, and technologists making decisions at the same speed on the same facts. Run them as separate suppliers reporting to a busy client and the case moves at the pace of the slowest email thread. A fusion cell is one team with a single decision authority, shared evidence, and a declared operational tempo.

## Components
**Core roles:**
| Role | Responsibility | Availability |
|---|---|---|
| Lead counsel | Legal strategy, client interface | 24/7 during the critical phase |
| Forensic analyst | Transaction tracing, technical analysis | 24/7 during the critical phase |
| Local counsel, per jurisdiction | Jurisdiction-specific filings, court appearances | Business hours plus emergency |
| Technical expert | Protocol mechanics, expert testimony | On call |
| Client representative | Decision authority, resource allocation | Regular briefings |
| Communications, if public | Media management, narrative control | As needed |

Extend with a law enforcement liaison, regulatory specialists, exchange relationship managers, and data privacy counsel as the case demands.

**Command and control:** one named decision authority with clear escalation paths, a formal approval process for critical decisions, secure channels agreed in advance, and a defined reporting cadence and format.

**Information management:** centralise evidence and strategy in one secure repository, apply need-to-know within the cell, use standardised briefing templates, and keep a live case dashboard.

**Communications:** end-to-end encrypted messaging as primary, encrypted email with PGP verification as secondary, a pre-agreed voice protocol with identity verification for emergencies, and scheduled secure briefings for the client.

**Operational tempo:**
- Hours 0 to 72: continuous operation on 8-hour rotating shifts.
- After 72 hours: 12-hour operational periods with formal handovers.
- Monitoring phase: scheduled check-ins with alert-based escalation.

**Cross-border coordination:** harmonise strategy across jurisdictions, assess the risk of conflicting orders, build a filing coordination timeline, set one evidence standard that satisfies every jurisdiction involved, and fix a single time reference (normally UTC) for the whole cell.

## When to use
Any recovery running in more than one jurisdiction, or any matter large enough to involve outside counsel plus external forensics. Below that scale the structure is overhead.

## Avoid when
Do not staff a fusion cell for a case whose budget cannot sustain it. The resource allocation tiers exist precisely to stop a full cell being stood up for a loss that cannot carry the cost.
