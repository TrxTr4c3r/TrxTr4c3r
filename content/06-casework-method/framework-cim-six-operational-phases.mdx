# The Six Operational Phases of the Cryptocurrency Investigation Methodology

<span style={{ fontFamily: 'var(--font-space-mono), monospace', fontSize: 13, opacity: 0.7 }}>`framework`</span>

## Core idea
CIM splits a crypto investigation into six sequential phases, each with one objective and one primary deliverable, so that "where am I in this case" always has an answer. The ordering carries the discipline: evidence is preserved before it is analysed, on-chain activity is analysed before it is correlated with off-chain information, and nothing is communicated until it has been assessed against typology and confidence.

## Components
1. **Initiate.** Receive, evaluate, document, and formally open the investigation. Activities: case intake, initial assessment, jurisdiction review, case documentation, case assignment. Deliverable: an established investigative case file.
2. **Preserve.** Collect, preserve, organise, and document evidence. Activities: evidence collection, evidence inventory, metadata documentation, preservation, chain of custody. Deliverable: a complete evidence inventory that later analysis can rest on.
3. **Analyze.** Examine blockchain activity and record what is observable. Activities: wallet analysis, transaction analysis, fund tracing, explorer review, smart contract observations. Deliverable: documented blockchain findings supported by observable evidence.
4. **Correlate.** Integrate the on-chain picture with everything off-chain. Activities: attribution analysis, OSINT collection, exchange and VASP review, cross-source corroboration. Deliverable: an evidence-supported analytical picture.
5. **Assess.** Place the findings against recognised typologies and state how confident you actually are. Activities: typology assessment, attribution confidence evaluation, alternative hypothesis review, identification of investigative limitations. Deliverable: an evidence-based analytical assessment.
6. **Communicate.** Write it up so somebody else can follow it. Activities: report planning, evidence organisation, analytical writing, quality assurance review, final documentation. Deliverable: a report fit for supervisory review or case presentation.

## When to use
As the spine for any investigation that will be reviewed by someone other than you: a supervisor, a prosecutor, a compliance committee, or a court. It is also the fastest way to diagnose a stalled case, because a case that will not move is usually being worked two phases ahead of the phase it actually completed.

## Avoid when
Investigations rarely run cleanly forward, and CIM does not pretend otherwise. Phase 6 output frequently sends you back to Phase 3. Treat the sequence as an ordering of dependencies, not a one-way gate, and do not delay preservation because you are "still in intake": new evidence arrives at every phase and is preserved on arrival.

## Example
A pig-butchering referral arrives by email. Initiate: open the case, record the referral channel and the reported loss, confirm jurisdiction. Preserve: save the original complaint, the victim's screenshots, and both deposit addresses exactly as received, then start an evidence inventory. Analyze: review each address on the correct explorer and trace the two legs to a co-mingling hub. Correlate: pull OSINT on the platform domain and identify the exchange holding the hub's downstream deposits. Assess: the indicator set matches investment fraud rather than money laundering as the predicate, and hub attribution rates Moderate pending exchange records. Communicate: write the report, stating what the evidence does not establish.
