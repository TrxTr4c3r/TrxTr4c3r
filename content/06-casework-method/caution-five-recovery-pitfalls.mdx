# Five Recovery Pitfalls

<span style={{ fontFamily: 'var(--font-space-mono), monospace', fontSize: 13, opacity: 0.7 }}>`caution`</span>

## Core idea
Recoveries rarely fail because the trace was wrong. They fail on timing, forum, evidence quality, exchange handling, and money spent in the wrong place. All five are visible in advance, which makes them worth checking as a list rather than learning individually.

## Components
**1. Delayed response.** Shows up as internal approvals holding up filings, response tracks run in sequence instead of in parallel, too much evidence gathered before the first freeze attempt, and technical analysis paralysis. Prevent it with pre-approved emergency protocols, clear authorisation thresholds, template documents, and a lower evidence bar for the initial action than for the follow-up. If it has already happened, shift to preservation, broadcast alerts to exchanges widely, concentrate on whatever choke points remain, and consider negotiating a voluntary return.

**2. Jurisdictional misalignment.** Shows up as orders obtained where they cannot be enforced, major exchange jurisdictions left uncovered, conflicting orders across systems, and legal spend in the wrong forum. Prevent it by predicting asset flow before choosing venues, keeping an exchange jurisdiction reference current, coordinating multi-jurisdiction filings, and prioritising enforcement effectiveness over legal elegance. If it has already happened, reassess against where the assets actually are now and look at mutual recognition applications.

**3. Technical evidence inadequacy.** Shows up as chain of custody breaks, explanations pitched over a non-specialist court's head, unverified explorer screenshots, and missing transaction hash verification. Prevent it with forensic-grade collection, court-ready templates for blockchain mechanics, expert declarations for technical validation, and original-format data held with verification hashes. If it has already happened, get independent verification of what exists and file supplemental technical declarations.

**4. Exchange relationship failures.** Shows up as over-reliance on formal process against venues that ignore it, thin initial freeze requests, regulatory leverage left unused, and inconsistent follow-up. Prevent it with a maintained contact database including escalation paths, relationships built before the incident, exchange-specific package templates, and a follow-up protocol with escalation triggers. If it has already happened, activate secondary pressure through regulators and banking partners.

**5. Resource misallocation.** Shows up as heavy spend on low-probability paths, underinvestment where recovery was actually likely, viable channels abandoned too early, and strategy that never adjusts as the assets move. Prevent it with stage gates, a probability-adjusted allocation model, and mandatory reassessment at fixed intervals.

## When to use
As a pre-mortem at the start of a recovery, and again at each stage gate.
