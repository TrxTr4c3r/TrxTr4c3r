# Recovery Budget Tiers by Asset Value

<span style={{ fontFamily: 'var(--font-space-mono), monospace', fontSize: 13, opacity: 0.7 }}>`framework`</span>

## Core idea
Recovery spend scales with the loss, but not linearly. Small losses need a larger share of their own value to chase, and at some point the share needed exceeds what recovery would return. Setting the tier at intake gives the client a defensible number and gives the case an honest floor.

## Components
| Asset value | Suggested budget | Where the money goes |
|---|---|---|
| Under $100,000 | 15 to 25% of asset value | Focused exchange outreach, limited legal action |
| $100,000 to $1M | 10 to 15% | Targeted legal action in one or two jurisdictions, forensic tracing |
| $1M to $10M | 5 to 10% | Multi-jurisdiction approach, full fusion cell |
| Over $10M | 2 to 5% | Comprehensive global strategy, extended team |

**Run it as a stage gate, not a lump sum:**
- Set recovery probability metrics up front and say what they are.
- Define continue and abandon criteria before spending, so the abandon decision is a plan rather than a defeat.
- Reassess strategy at fixed intervals, not only when something goes wrong.
- Document the risk and reward calculation behind every major expenditure, because the client will ask later and so may their insurer.

## When to use
At intake, when the loss is scoped and before committing to any filing. Revisit at each stage gate as the asset location changes.

## Avoid when
Do not treat the percentages as a quote. They are a proportionality guide from one practice's field experience, and actual cost swings hard on jurisdiction count, whether the assets are already at a compliant venue, and whether the counterparty is contactable at all.

## Example
A $60,000 theft sits in the bottom tier, so the realistic budget is roughly $9,000 to $15,000. That funds a trace and a serious exchange outreach effort. It does not fund a filing in two jurisdictions, and saying so at intake is more useful to the victim than discovering it after the money is spent.
