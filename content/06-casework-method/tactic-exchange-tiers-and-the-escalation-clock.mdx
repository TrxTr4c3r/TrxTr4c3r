# Exchange Tiers and the Escalation Clock

<span style={{ fontFamily: 'var(--font-space-mono), monospace', fontSize: 13, opacity: 0.7 }}>`tactic`</span>

## Core idea
Not every exchange responds to the same pressure. Sort the target by how regulated it is, then match the approach to the tier and put a clock on the escalation, because a freeze request with no escalation trigger just sits in a queue while the funds move.

## Components
**Tier the exchange first:**
- **Tier 1, regulated major.** Formal legal process works. Bring a court order and a suspicious activity filing.
- **Tier 2, regulated regional.** Direct compliance contact backed by legal process. Bring a freeze declaration and the chain analysis.
- **Tier 3, limited regulation.** Heightened pressure through several channels at once. Bring an expanded evidence package, and consider a media strategy.
- **Non-compliant, jurisdiction-evading.** Formal process alone will not land. Escalate to regulators and apply indirect pressure through banking and infrastructure partners, with enforcement referrals and public exposure as the levers.

**Send everything at once, not in sequence:**
1. Contact the compliance team directly.
2. Submit simultaneously: the formal freeze request letter, the evidence package with the transaction tracing, any court orders already obtained, and law enforcement contact details.

**Then run the clock:**
- No response after 4 hours, escalate to legal counsel.
- No response after 12 hours, notify the regulator.

**Finding the exchange in the first place:** watch for movement toward known deposit addresses, read gas fee and withdrawal patterns for an exchange footprint, look for exchange-specific token patterns in the flow, and watch the mempool for pending transactions to exchange wallets.

## When to use
The moment traced funds reach or approach a centralised venue and a freeze is still possible.

## Avoid when
Do not open with public exposure or media pressure against a Tier 1 or Tier 2 venue. It burns a working relationship you will need on the next case, and those tiers respond to process anyway. Save it for the venues where nothing else reaches.

## Example
Funds land at a regional exchange. Compliance gets the full package at 10:00. Nothing back by 14:00, so counsel takes over the thread. Still nothing by 22:00, so the licensing regulator is notified, which is usually the message the compliance team actually answers.
